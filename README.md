# Liam Moran
**PhD Student | Computational Neuroscience | Neuroendocrine Modelling**

I'm a PhD student in the **Broberger Lab** at Stockholm University. My research focuses on understanding how hypothalamic dopamine neurons—especially those in the **TIDA network**—interact electrically to regulate hormone release. I’m interested in how small neural networks synchronise robustly despite biological noise, and I will **computational modelling** to bridge the gap between cellular physiology and emergent circuit dynamics.

---

## About Me
I’m driven by a fascination with **emergent phenomena**, **determinism**, and **dynamic systems**, both within the brain and in broader contexts. My research focuses on:

- **Neuroendocrine Circuits**: Investigating how hypothalamic dopamine neurons regulate hormone release.
- **TIDA Network**: Understanding how gap junction connectivity and intrinsic ion currents generate stable, rhythmic activity.
- **Emergent Dynamics**: Exploring how simple local rules lead to robust global synchronisation in neural circuits.

### Key Questions I Explore:
- How does **network architecture** shape stability and adaptability?
- How do neural circuits encode **behavioural and hormonal states**?
- How do systems reorganise under **perturbation**, such as pharmacological modulation or environmental changes?

## Skills & Techniques
### Experimental
- **Electrophysiology**: Measuring the electrical activity of neurons to study intrinsic properties and network dynamics.
- **Organotypic Culture**: Maintaining neural circuits in a controlled environment for extended observation.
- **Immunohistochemistry**: Visualizing specific proteins and neuronal populations.

### Computational
- **Conductance-Based Modeling**: Simulating neural circuits to investigate how coupling strength, ion currents, and feedback shape network synchrony.
- **Dynamical Systems Analysis**: Exploring stability, oscillations, and bifurcations in neural networks.
- **Data Analysis**: Using Python and MATLAB for analyzing electrophysiological recordings and imaging data.

## Computational Modeling Philosophy
I believe that **computational modeling** is a powerful tool to bridge the gap between experimental data and theoretical understanding. By integrating experimental results with simulations, I aim to:
- Uncover the principles that govern robust and adaptable neural communication.
- Predict how neural circuits respond to perturbations.
- Generate testable hypotheses to guide future experiments.
